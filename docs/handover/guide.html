CHAPTER 01

Your website at a glance

Your journal website is built on Open Journal Systems (OJS) 3.5, the software used by tens of thousands of scholarly journals. It has been given its own design, a simpler admin panel, clickable references and author pages.

What the website does

FeatureWhat it means for you
Online submissionAuthors register and send manuscripts through a guided form. You receive them in the admin panel.
Peer reviewDouble-anonymous review with invitations, deadlines and automatic reminders to reviewers.
Issues and archiveArticles are published in issues (volume, number, year) with a cover. All past issues stay in the archive.
PDF and HTML full textEvery article has a PDF that opens in the browser. Optionally, the full text can also be shown on the article page.
Clickable referencesCitations such as [1] or (Smith, 2020) link to the reference list and show the reference when the reader points at them.
Author pagesEvery published author gets a public page with photo, affiliation, ORCID, biography and articles.
Identifiers and indexingDOIs through Crossref, ORCID iDs, Google Scholar metadata and OAI-PMH, which indexing services use to harvest your articles.
Email notificationsAuthors, reviewers and editors receive emails automatically at each step.

Who does what

RoleWhat they can do
Administrator (you)Everything, including site-wide settings. This is the account on the cover.
Journal managerAll submissions, issues, settings, users and statistics of the journal.
Journal editorThe editor-in-chief: all submissions and editorial decisions.
Section editorOnly the submissions assigned to them.
ReviewerTheir review invitations and review forms.
AuthorTheir own submissions, messages from the editors and their public profile.
ReaderAnyone can read and download articles without an account.

How an article gets published

Every article passes through the same six steps. You will see these colours throughout the admin panel.

  1. SubmissionAn editor checks scope and format.
  2. Peer reviewReviewers assess the manuscript.
  3. CopyeditingLanguage and style are polished.
  4. ProductionThe final PDF (and HTML) is prepared.
  5. ScheduledThe article is placed in an issue.
  6. PublishedThe issue goes live on the website.

Authors submit; editors publish. An article only appears on the website after an editor publishes the issue it belongs to.

Signing in and the Home page

Everything you manage happens in the admin panel. You reach it by signing in on the website.

Sign in

  1. Open {{SITE}} and click Login at the top right.
  2. Enter your username and password (see the cover) and click Login.
  3. You arrive on the Home page of the admin panel.
  1. Username (or your email address).
  2. Password.
  3. The Login button.
  4. Forgot your password? sends a reset link to your email.
Getting back to the admin panel

When you are on the public website and signed in, open your username at the top right and choose Dashboard. The admin panel also works on a phone; the menu then sits above the page.

The Home page

Home shows what needs your attention today. Every number is a link that opens the matching list of submissions.

  1. Menu. Home, Submissions, Issues, Announcements, DOIs, Settings, Statistics, Tools and Administration.
  2. Needs your attention. New submissions without an editor, submissions ready for a decision, revisions from authors and overdue reviews.
  3. Publishing pipeline. How many submissions are in each of the six steps.
  4. Recent submissions with their current status. Click a title to open it.
  5. Current issue, with links to view it on the website or manage issues.
  6. Shortcuts to common tasks.
  7. View website opens the public site in a new tab.
  8. New submission lets you submit a manuscript on someone's behalf.

CHAPTER 04

Receiving new submissions

When an author submits a manuscript, you receive an email and it appears under New submissions on Home. The next step is a first check by an editor.

The Submissions list

  1. Ready-made lists with counts: Needs editor, Awaiting reviews, Reviews overdue, each stage, Published and more.
  2. Filters: by section, assigned editor, issue or days since last activity.
  3. Search by title, author or ID.
  4. Assign Editor gives the submission to an editor.
  5. View opens the submission.

Checking a new submission

  1. The submission's menu. Workflow holds the steps (Submission, Review, Copyediting, Production); Publication holds the article details (title and abstract, contributors, metadata, references, galleys, issue).
  2. The files the author uploaded. Click a file to download it.
  3. Send for Review moves it to peer review (chapter 5).
  4. Decline Submission if it is out of scope. The author is notified by email.
  5. Assign adds an editor (or other participant) to this submission.
  1. Open the submission from New submissions on Home or from Submissions › Needs editor.
  2. Under Participants, click Assign and choose the handling editor.
  3. Check the manuscript against the journal's scope and author guidelines. Make sure the author's name is not inside the manuscript file, because review is anonymous.
  4. Click Send for Review, Accept and Skip Review (for editorials, for example) or Decline Submission.
Discussions

Use Add discussion on any stage to message the author or other participants. Messages are sent by email and kept with the submission.

05

CHAPTER 05

Peer review

Reviewers are invited by email, respond online and submit their review through a form. You then record a decision.

Recording a decision

Every decision (Send for Review, Accept, Request Revisions, Decline) opens the same kind of page: first an email to the author, then the files to carry forward.

  1. The steps of the decision: notify the authors, then select the files.
  2. The email to the author, pre-written from a template. Edit it freely; it is only sent when you click Record Decision.
Anonymous review

Reviewers and authors do not see each other's names. When you share reviews with the author, only the comments meant for the author are included.

The Review stage

  1. Add Reviewer: choose a reviewer or create a new one, set the deadlines and send the invitation.
  2. Request Revisions asks the author for changes.
  3. Accept Submission moves it to copyediting.
  4. Decline Submission.
  1. Click Add Reviewer. Search your reviewers by name or interest, or create a new reviewer account.
  2. Check the deadlines. By default reviewers have 7 days to respond and 21 days to complete the review. Reminders are sent automatically.
  3. Follow progress in the Reviewers panel; you receive an email when a review is submitted.
  4. When enough reviews are in, read them and record your decision. If you request revisions, the author uploads a revised file on the same page, and you can start a new review round with Create New Review Round.
06

CHAPTER 06

Copyediting and production

After acceptance, the manuscript is polished and turned into the files readers will see: a PDF and, optionally, an HTML full text.

Copyediting

  1. Send To Production once the copyedited file is ready.
  2. Participants: assign a copyeditor here, or upload the edited file yourself under Copyedited Files.

Production: galleys

A galley is a file readers see on the article page. Every article needs a PDF galley; an HTML galley is optional and shows the full text on the page, with clickable citations.

  1. Publication › Galleys in the submission's menu.
  2. Add galley: label it PDF (or Full Text for HTML) and upload the file.
  3. Schedule For Publication places the article in an issue (chapter 7).
  1. Use a text-based PDF (not a scan) with the title and authors on the first page. Google Scholar needs this.
  2. Before scheduling, check each part of Publication: Title & Abstract, Contributors (with ORCID iDs), Metadata (keywords), References, Permissions & Disclosure and Issue.
  3. Click Schedule For Publication and choose the issue.

References

  1. Publication › References.
  2. Paste the reference list, one reference per line. Include the DOI whenever there is one (for example https://doi.org/10.1000/xyz123).

The website then numbers the references, links every DOI and web address, and adds Google Scholar and Crossref look-up links to each reference. Keywords become search links automatically.

HTML full text (optional)

To show the full text on the article page, add a galley labelled Full Text with an HTML file. Keep the HTML simple:

  • Use level-2 and level-3 headings (1. Introduction, 2.1 Animals). They build the On this page menu automatically.
  • Write citations as [1], [1, 2], [3–5] or (Smith et al., 2020). They become clickable automatically.
  • Upload images as additional files of the same galley, with the same file names used in the HTML.
  • From Microsoft Word: File › Save as › Web Page, Filtered, then delete the title, author list and abstract from the file, because the page already shows them.

CHAPTER 07

Issues: create and publish

Articles go live together when you publish their issue. Prepare the next issue in advance, schedule articles into it, then publish it with one click.

  1. Future Issues: issues you are preparing.
  2. Back Issues: issues already published.
  3. Create Issue.

Create an issue

  1. Volume.
  2. Number.
  3. Year.
  4. Cover image: portrait, about 600 × 800 pixels. It appears on the home page, the issue page, the archive and article pages.
  5. Save.

Publish an issue

  1. Schedule each article into the issue from its Production stage (chapter 6).
  2. Go to Issues › Future Issues and open the issue. Check the table of contents and the order of the articles.
  3. Click Publish Issue. All its articles go online at once, DOIs are registered (if set up, chapter 11) and the issue becomes the current issue on the home page.
Correcting a published article

Open the article and click Create New Version in Publication. Make your changes and publish the new version; the website keeps the earlier version available for transparency.

CHAPTER 08

Authors and the author area

Authors have their own area in the admin panel, where they submit manuscripts, follow them and keep their public profile up to date. This chapter shows what they see, so you can guide them.

Registering and submitting

  1. The author clicks Register on the website, fills in the form and confirms their email address.
  2. They click Submit Manuscript on the home page (or New submission in their area).
  3. They enter the title, choose a section and confirm the submission checklist, then go through Details (abstract, keywords, references), Upload Files, Contributors, For the Editors and Review, and submit.
  4. They can save and finish later; unfinished submissions are kept as drafts.

The author's Home page

  1. New submission.
  2. Their submissions with a plain status: Draft, Submitted, In review, Copyediting, Production, Scheduled, Published or Declined.
  3. A checklist for their public profile: photo, biography, affiliation, ORCID iD and website.
  4. A link to their public author page (once they have published).

Photo and biography

  1. In Profile › Public, the profile image…
  2. …is uploaded with Upload File (a square photo of at least 150 × 150 pixels). The biography and website go below it.
How author pages are matched

An author's page collects all their published articles. Articles are matched by ORCID iD, or otherwise by email address. The photo and biography come from the author's account with the same email address. Ask authors to add their ORCID iD to every submission.

CHAPTER 09

Users, roles and the editorial team

Everyone who uses the admin panel has an account and one or more roles. You manage them in Settings › Users & Roles.

  1. Invite to a role: enter a person's email address and choose a role (for example Section editor or Reviewer). They receive an invitation to accept.
  2. Search users by name, email or role.
  3. Each user with their roles, start date and affiliation.
  4. The ⋯ menu: edit the user's details and roles, or email them.

Roles

The editorial team page

The Editorial Team page on the website lists people in the editorial roles (Journal editor, Section editor, Editorial Board Member), with their affiliation. To keep it up to date:

  1. Give each board member an account with the right role (invite them, as above).
  2. Make sure their profile has their affiliation and, ideally, their ORCID iD.
  3. Set the order of the roles on the page in Settings › Website › Appearance › Editorial Masthead.

CHAPTER 11

DOIs and statistics

A DOI is a permanent link to an article, required by most indexing services. Statistics show how often your articles are read and downloaded.

DOIs

  1. Become a member of Crossref (crossref.org) to receive your DOI prefix and login.
  2. Enter the prefix in Settings › Distribution › DOIs. DOIs are then assigned automatically when articles are published.
  3. Enter your Crossref login under Registration on the same tab, so DOIs and reference lists are registered automatically.

CHAPTER 12

Your password and account

Your login is on the cover of this guide. You can change the password whenever you like; we recommend doing so after your first sign-in and keeping it in a password manager.

Change your password

  1. Click your initials at the top right of the admin panel and choose Edit Profile.
  2. Open the Password tab.
  3. Enter your current password, then the new password twice, and click Save.
  1. Current password.
  2. New password (at least 6 characters; longer is safer).
  3. Repeat the new password.
  4. Save.

The same profile has your name, email address, affiliation and notification preferences. If you forget your password, use Forgot your password? on the login page; a reset link is sent to your email address.

If a change doesn't appear on the website

The website keeps copies of pages to stay fast. If a change to the settings doesn't show, clear them:

  1. Administration › Delete Data Caches.
  2. Delete Template Cache. Then reload the website page.
Keep your account safe

Use the administrator account for administration only. For day-to-day editorial work, invite each editor with their own account (chapter 9), so everyone signs in with their own name and the activity log shows who did what.

Common questions

Can authors publish their own articles?
No. Authors submit; editors publish after peer review. This is what makes the journal peer-reviewed, and indexing services such as DOAJ and Scopus require it. For sections without review, such as editorials, use Accept and Skip Review.
A change I made doesn't show on the website.
Clear the caches (chapter 12) and reload the page.
An author's photo doesn't appear on their author page.
The photo comes from the author's own account (Profile › Public). The email address in the account must match the one on the article, or the article must have the author's ORCID iD.
A reviewer didn't receive the invitation.
Ask them to check their spam folder. In the Reviewers panel of the submission you can resend the request or email the reviewer directly.
How do I correct a published article?
Open it, click Create New Version in Publication, make the change and publish the new version (chapter 7).
How do I remove the sample content?
The sample articles and people shown in this guide are only for illustration. Your website contains your own journal; add your issues and articles as described in chapters 4 to 7.
Will the journal be indexed in Scopus, Web of Science or DOAJ?
The website meets the technical requirements of these services (metadata, DOIs, open licences, OAI-PMH). Acceptance is decided by each service, based mainly on the journal's content, publishing regularity and history. DOAJ usually expects about a year of publishing, and Scopus about two years.
